A Building Without a Business Case

Berkeley has committed roughly $15.1 million to build the African American Holistic Resource Center, due to open in April 2028. In 2020 the Council asked what it would cost to run. No answer to that question has been located, and the project advanced through every subsequent gate without one.

Material Governance Failure · scored August 2026 · last revised August 2026

This page documents a governance failure scored under this project’s published standard. It is a record of process, not a verdict on the facility. Whether Berkeley should build an African American Holistic Resource Center is a question this page does not answer and does not need to answer. The finding concerns what Council knew about the cost of operating it, and when.

The finding in one paragraph

In March 2020 Council directed staff to report on the ongoing operating cost of the proposed center. No responsive report has been located. The project then advanced through roughly $15.1 million in capital commitments and two $300,000 service contracts — both adopted on the consent calendar without discussion — while the operator, service model, annual operating budget, committed outside revenue, City recurring exposure and performance standards remained unestablished on the record. Across sixty-one meeting transcripts covering December 2024 to July 2026, no councilmember raised operating cost, operator identity or staffing model for this facility.

What the 2018 study actually established

The feasibility study is the document most often cited as having answered the operating-cost question. It is worth reading what it says, because it discloses its own limits on the same page.

“Providing an annual operational budget for a new agency or program requires some room for uncertainties, because there are many unknowns associated with the operation of a new agency. Sometimes operational budgets are created from a percentage of the hard cost of a construction project. The projected annual budget for the AAHRC will range from $1.5 – $2 million dollars.”

AAHRC feasibility study, “Projected Annual Operational Budget”

The study states a number. It does not derive that number from a service model — from who would staff the building, what hours it would keep, what programs it would run, or how many people it would serve. It says, in the sentence immediately preceding the figure, that such budgets are sometimes produced as a percentage of construction cost.

That is a disclosure, not a concealment. The study is candid that it is estimating under uncertainty. But it means the $1.5–$2 million figure cannot bear the weight of an operating plan, and a reader of the document would see that.

What Council asked for, and did not get

The clearest evidence that Council itself found the 2018 estimate insufficient is that Council asked for more.

In March 2020 it directed the City Manager to evaluate a proposed site and report on implementation steps, physical improvements, costs and ongoing operating costs. That direction is only necessary if the existing estimate was not adequate for a site-specific decision.

No responsive report has been located. A California Public Records Act request for the resulting work is outstanding as of this writing. If no responsive record exists, that absence is itself the finding: Council asked a question, and the project proceeded through six years and roughly $15.1 million without the answer arriving.

The gap was still open in the document Council adopted

The most recent evidence is also the plainest. The FY2027–28 budget, adopted June 23, 2026, lists strategic priority projects in a table. Each row carries a department, a description, a start and end date, and an estimated budget.

The row for a facility opening in April 2028 and running “Ongoing” says TBD. The budget document disclosed the gap accurately. It was adopted as presented.

What each member did, and when

Two gates fall inside this project’s vote corpus: the consent contracts of September 9 and October 14, 2025. At both, the item was adopted in the consent motion. Neither was pulled. Neither produced a roll call.

Eight members were present at both and are recorded uniformly as notified non-actors: Kesarwani, Taplin, Tregub, O’Keefe, Blackaby, Lunaparra, Humbert and Ishii. Bartlett is recorded as a passive non-participant at these gates — not because his responsibility is lesser, but because these are gates he did not attend.

The uniformity is the point. The record differentiates no roles here. No member of this Council originated the project, none objected, none moved to reverse, and none asked the operating question at either gate. A body that acted as one unit failed as one unit.

Across sixty-one meeting transcripts spanning December 2024 to July 2026, the AAHRC is mentioned five times in attributed speech. The number of members raising operating cost, operator identity or staffing model is zero.

The one member who answered

In August 2026 a Berkeley resident wrote to council offices asking four narrow questions: what annual operating cost the member understood the facility would impose; what ongoing funding source would cover it; whether the member reviewed an analysis addressing those questions before the latest capital commitment; and whether Council should require a sustainable operating plan before approving a new facility.

Councilmember Igor Tregub replied. He is named here for one reason, and it is not that his conduct was worse: he is the only member who answered, which makes his the only state of knowledge documented on the record. The reply is evidence of the council-wide omission, not a distinction in his conduct.

On the general mechanism he confirmed the diagnosis:

“If I am reading the subtext of your question correctly, you are suggesting that the analyses provided by the City may not fully capture such lifecycle costs — most likely because operational and maintenance costs come out of a different pot of the budget than funds allocated toward design, engineering, and construction. I believe that this concern is well-placed, will discuss it with the City Manager the next time that we meet, and will update you as I’m able.”

Councilmember Igor Tregub, written reply, August 2026

Asked what the facility would cost annually to run, he did not supply a figure; he supplied the capital funding history instead, all of it predating his June 2024 swearing-in. Asked what would fund it, he undertook to confirm with the City Manager whether there are operating costs borne by the City at all. Asked whether he had reviewed an analysis, he cited the budget line quoted above — the one reading “Budget TBD.”

That is the answer this page rests on, and it is worth stating precisely what it shows. It is not evasion; it is a candid account of the information available. A member was asked what a $15.1 million building will cost to operate, and the best answer the record afforded him was a budget line that says the figure has not been determined.

What this does and does not establish

Established

That Council asked for an operating-cost analysis in March 2020 and that no responsive report has been located. That the 2018 study’s figure is unsubstantiated by a service model, on the study’s own account. That roughly $15.1 million in capital and two $300,000 service contracts advanced regardless. That both service contracts passed on consent without discussion. That no member raised the operating question in sixty-one transcripts. That the budget adopted in June 2026 still recorded the operating budget as TBD.

Not established: that any member had actual notice

Notice here is constructive, not actual. The finding is that formal City documents on a project of this scale — a commissioned feasibility study, Council’s own direction, the budget it adopted — are documents ordinary office-level review would encounter. No record establishes that any particular member encountered the deficiency before voting. That distinction is why this event carries the notice score it does.

Not established: any consequence to any member’s grade

This event is scored critical, raw severity 13 of 14. Applied at the notified non-actor multiplier it reaches an effective 7.8, below the 8.0 threshold at which per-member consequences attach. It produces no composite adjustment and no grade ceiling for any member. It is recorded because it happened, not because it moves a score. Whether a uniform failure by nine members should fall below a threshold calibrated on differentiated failures is an open question this project has recorded rather than resolved.

Not claimed: that the facility should not be built

This page takes no position on whether Berkeley should build an AAHRC. The 2011 community report and the 2016 Health Commission recommendation describe a need this project does not evaluate. A facility can be worth building and still be committed to without an operating plan.

Not claimed: bad faith, or motive of any kind

No concealment is alleged and none is evident. Every document described here is public. Consent placement follows the City’s ordinary agenda practice. Nothing here suggests the project exists to serve any interest other than the one it states. Where a member has been named, the naming carries conduct and its recurrence only.

Not claimed: that origin responsibility rests with this Council

Most of the capital history predates the current Council’s terms, and several members took office in 2024. Origin responsibility is not at issue for them. Continuation responsibility is: each gate after they took office was an occasion to ask.

Bounded to what was examined

The transcript claim covers the sixty-one meeting transcripts in this project’s corpus, December 2024 to July 2026, and the annotated agenda record. Pre-2024 gate participation is outstanding evidence. The original 2020–2023 staff reports have not been obtained; whether any needs assessment or provider diagnosis exists, and whether any alternative to a building was ever costed, are open research questions. Silence in this record is not a verdict.

Why this is recorded as a governance failure

Not because the money was misspent. Nothing here shows that.

Because a completed municipal building creates an obligation that is not practically reversible. Once it opens in April 2028 it will need staff, utilities and maintenance every year thereafter, and the City’s own estimate for that is $1.5–$2 million annually against a General Fund already carrying a structural deficit. The moment to establish who operates it, what it does, and what recurring exposure the City accepts was before construction, not after.

Council recognized that in March 2020 and asked the right question. The failure is that nobody noticed the answer never came, through six years and six gates — and that the two most recent gates were adopted in a motion that does not stop for questions.
