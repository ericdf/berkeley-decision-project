“Robust”: We’ve Been Down This Bumpy Road Before
Berkeley is reusing a word that sounded reassuring under T1 but supplied no measurable accountability standard.

4
Who Pays the Staff?
Bonds can reasonably pay for temporary staff needed to deliver extra projects. But if the work persists after the bond, voters should know the appropriate recurring operating source that will carry it. Bond money should add incremental delivery capacity, not merely relocate ordinary staffing costs among funds. If the jobs remain after the bond ends, voters should ask whether Measure U is buying more infrastructure or shifting costs the City will continue to carry elsewhere.
Available now   7 questions · 7 points
5
Measure U: Did Berkeley Learn from T1?
Measure U is not a fresh governance model. It is a larger authorization built on substantially the same architecture that allowed T1’s voter-facing priorities to remain mutable.
Available now   5 questions · 5 points
6
Could Berkeley Have Written a Better Bond?
The choice is not “Measure U or no infrastructure.” Berkeley could preserve flexibility while making material changes measurable, visible, and auditable. Because Measure U does not do so and cannot now be rewritten before November, rejection is the appropriate decision for a voter who treats start/stop/continue discipline as essential.
